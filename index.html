Is it luck? The overfitting checks

Backtests flatter whoever runs them. edgeterm counts every variant it tried and runs the standard tests against itself, using backtest-autopsy.

Could luck alone explain the result, given that 11 variants were tried? Above 0.95 says no. It passes, but only on the years the rule was designed on.

In euros, after costs and tax

What the numbers mean for a real French saver, compared with the tax-free Livret A.

  • On €5,000, the trend strategy made +€618 a year over 2018–2026 (+€424 after the 31.4 % flat tax), and −€12 a year on the unseen days. The Livret A paid €85, tax-free.
  • Doubling or tripling every trading cost takes the yearly return from +12.4 % to +11.2 % and +10.0 %. Costs are not what breaks it.
  • If swaps from crypto to stablecoins become taxable (a 2027 bill, still pending), the yearly return falls to +8.3 %.
  • Funding carry (long spot, short perpetual) earned +0.7 % on BTC, +0.3 % on ETH and −1.8 % on SOL over the last year, all below the Livret A's 1.7 %. It stays in paper mode.

How it works

A Python terminal and a local web dashboard over one engine.

Pure-math signals

A volatility-scaled ensemble of nine Donchian breakouts, long or flat, spot only. No news, no macro, no sentiment, no LLM in the loop.

Honest backtest

Each coin's measured cost (fee, half-spread, depth impact), funding at its real timestamps, a true holdout, walk-forward, bootstrap confidence intervals, and a test that changing future prices never changes a past decision.

Paper first, live behind locks

The paper loop runs 24/7 against live order books. Live trading needs an explicit switch, 28 days of paper journal, a key the exchange reports as trade-only, and a −5 % daily kill-switch.

Every number explained

Today's signals with the calculation behind each one, a chart that replays every buy and sell on any coin (BTC back to 2012), and a trade calculator with break-even after costs and tax.

Built for where it runs

Data from Binance's public history; execution on OKX EU, USDC pairs, because Binance stopped serving French residents in July 2026.

Tested

50 tests cover the math, costs, the tax ledger, look-ahead, funding timestamps, the paper loop, the kill-switch and the live locks.

Numbers come from edgeterm's own research report, generated on data up to . Past results, simulated or not, say nothing certain about the future.
